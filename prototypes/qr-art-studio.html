How this works

A QR code's pixels are one long codeword stream: your URL, then free padding bytes, then Reed-Solomon check bytes derived from both. The solver treats every padding bit (and, optionally, URL letter-case bits — scheme and host are case-insensitive per RFC 3986) as a variable, and pins your highest-priority pixels exactly by Gauss-Jordan elimination over GF(2) — the QArt trick.

What the solver can't reach, the flip budget buys: modules deliberately set wrong, grouped per codeword (a codeword is a 2×4-ish blob — one flip pays for up to 8 pixels), spending at most the budget slider's share of each block's correction capacity. The meter shows exactly how much scanner headroom remains — that's your "will it still scan" gauge. Noise cells cost nothing: uncontrolled entropy reads as a mid-gray tone, so good designs use three tones, not two.

The decode check is a real one — format info, unmasking, de-interleaving and Berlekamp-Massey error correction — the same pipeline a scanner runs (engine verified against qrcode-generator and jsQR). Still: scan a print with a couple of phones before shipping a poster.
