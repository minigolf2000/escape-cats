How this works

Black and white pixels you paint are promises the tool must keep; noise pixels are surrendered to the code, which fills them with whatever static it needs. Behind the scenes a solver treats every spare bit of the payload as a variable and pins your pixels exactly (Gauss-Jordan over GF(2), the QArt trick), then spends a bounded slice of the code's Reed-Solomon error budget on any stragglers.

The meter shows that spend per internal block — headroom left is real scanner tolerance. When you out-paint the budget, nothing breaks: the code simply stops honoring your least-defensible pixels and marks them red. A background pass retries every mask and error-correction level and silently adopts a setting that honors more of your paint. The code in front of you always scans.

Scanned links may show odd capitalization (hexxygON.CoM) — the solver uses letter-case freedom in the scheme/host, which is case-insensitive by spec. Paths are never touched.
